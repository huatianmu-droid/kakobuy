Count the same thing before comparing quantities

An order quantity, a photograph of several objects and a parcel count can all be correct while describing different units. Write the unit beside every count.

Distinguish pieces from selling units

A pair of shoes is commonly sold as one pair, even though a photograph shows two shoes. A matching clothing set may contain more than one piece. Copy the listing’s quantity definition into your private record and keep it separate from the number of visible objects.

For an invented example, an order for two two-piece sets describes two selling units and four garment pieces. A photograph showing three garments does not yet establish a missing set: it may be incomplete, overlap another image or show only one part of a set. Record what is visible and what still needs confirmation.

Give each observation its own scope

  • Ordered: quantity, selling unit and variant as written on the order.
  • Received: the quantity confirmed by the receiving record and its source.
  • Photographed: distinct objects visible, with the image coverage stated.
  • Packed: items confirmed in the packing record, if available.
  • Parcels: shipping packages, which need not equal the item count.

Do not turn a photograph count into a received count unless the source establishes that the photo covers everything received. Two parcels can contain one order, and one parcel can contain several orders.

Avoid counting the same item twice

Front and back photographs often show the same garment. Use a private item label to link views rather than counting each image as another item. If you cannot tell whether two photos show different objects, mark that uncertainty instead of guessing from filenames or upload order.

When the quantity appears inconsistent, ask a focused question about the unit and coverage: does this image show all pieces for the ordered set? Keep the reply beside the observation. A clearer coverage statement may resolve the apparent discrepancy without changing the order.

Record a discrepancy without declaring its cause

Write the expected count, observed count and unit, then explain the evidence gap. Missing, not photographed and not yet checked are different statuses. Avoid describing loss, duplication or seller error until the relevant records support that conclusion.
